# SALES PITCH DECK
## Key Talking Points for Pitching to Skeptical Business Owners

**Purpose:** When pitching to a business owner who is skeptical, hesitant, or doesn't see the value — use these talking points to address their specific concerns.

---

## THE CORE PITCH (30 Seconds)

### The One-Liner

> "We build professional websites for businesses like yours — with online booking, loyalty programs, and gift cards included. Same functionality as big businesses, at a price you can afford."

### The Problem Statement

> "Most businesses in Paraguay run on WhatsApp. It's free, everyone's on it, and it works... until it doesn't. You get 200 messages, can't find the booking from Tuesday, and a new customer who wanted to book on Sunday already went to your competitor because you didn't respond at 11pm."

### The Solution Statement

> "What we do is give you a professional website — like having a receptionist online 24/7. Customers can book, see your services, pay, and get loyalty points — all without touching your WhatsApp."

### The Value Statement

> "You get more customers (because Google finds you), you serve them better (because bookings are organized), and they come back more (because of loyalty points). And it costs less than you think."

---

## OBJECTION HANDLING BY CATEGORY

---

### OBJECTION: "I Don't Need a Website"

**Response:**
> "Let me ask you this — when a new customer wants to find out about your business, where do they go?"

**Wait for answer.**

> "Facebook? Instagram? But here's the thing — 75% of Paraguayans search on Google first. And when they search, your competitors appear. You don't."

**Show:**
- Demo Google search for their industry in Asunción
- What the competitor's website looks like

**Close:**
> "You don't need a website — but your competitors with websites are getting customers you're not."

---

### OBJECTION: "WhatsApp Is Enough"

**Response:**
> "WhatsApp is great. We love WhatsApp. But here's what happens:"

**Tell a story:**
> "A customer texts you at 8pm asking about your services. You're cooking dinner, don't see it until the next morning. By then, they already booked with someone else."

> "Or this — a customer wants to book for Saturday. You say 'sure, come at 10am.' Saturday arrives, they don't show. You waited. That's a lost slot."

> "With a website, customers book when they want — 24/7. You get a notification, you confirm. Everything is tracked."

**The numbers:**
> "Businesses with online booking capture 40% more customers than those using WhatsApp only."

---

### OBJECTION: "It's Too Expensive"

**Response:**
> "Let me break it down. Plan Pro is G. 250.000 per month."

**Calculate with them:**
> "That's about G. 8.000 per day. Less than a coffee."

> "Now — how many customers do you lose per month because people can't find you online? How many bookings get lost in WhatsApp? How many hours do you spend responding to messages?"

> "We had a restaurant owner tell us: 'I was spending 5 hours a week on WhatsApp just answering questions about menus and hours. Now I don't.' That's 20 hours a month. What could you do with 20 hours?"

**ROI framework:**
> "If you get just ONE additional customer per week from Google — at G. 50.000 per service — that's G. 200.000 per month. The website pays for itself."

---

### OBJECTION: "I Don't Know How to Use Technology"

**Response:**
> "You don't need to know anything. That's our job."

**Explain the process:**
> "You give us: your photos, your services, your prices, your WhatsApp number. That's it. We build the whole thing."

> "After it's built, we show you how to make changes. Want to update your hours? Two clicks. Change a price? Done. Add a new service? We show you how."

> "It's like a phone — you don't know how the signal works, but you can make calls. Same here."

---

### OBJECTION: "My Business Is Different"

**Response:**
> "We know. That's why we have different templates for different industries."

**Show the variety:**
> "Peluquería? We have a template designed for beauty salons — with before/after galleries, booking for color appointments, loyalty for regular clients."

> "Restaurante? We have one with a digital menu, table reservations, and gift cards for special occasions."

> "Gimnasio? We have membership tiers, class booking, and progress tracking."

> "No matter what your business is, we have a starting point. Then we customize it for you."

---

### OBJECTION: "I Already Have a Facebook Page"

**Response:**
> "Facebook is great — and we're not replacing it. Think of it this way:"

**The algorithm problem:**
> "Your Facebook page is in your neighborhood. Your website is on Google. When someone searches 'best [your business] in Asunción,' do you appear?"

> "With Facebook alone, you depend on the algorithm. You post something, only 20% of your followers see it. You either pay to boost or hope people check in."

> "A website is yours. No algorithm. No boost. Just customers finding you."

**The professional image:**
> "Here's something we hear a lot: 'When a new client asks for my website, I just give them my WhatsApp.'"

> "A website is your business card. Your credibility. It says 'we're real, we're professional, we're here.'"

---

### OBJECTION: "I'll Do It Later"

**Response:**
> "I understand. But let me ask — when was the last time 'later' actually happened?"

**The cost of waiting:**
> "Every month without a website, you're losing customers to competitors who ARE online. Every week you're using WhatsApp for bookings is a week of disorganized chaos that could be simplified."

> "The businesses that started 6 months ago? They're already getting Google customers. They're already tracking their data. They're already building loyalty."

**Create urgency without pressure:**
> "We can have you live in 48 hours. What would it mean for your business to have online booking starting this weekend?"

---

### OBJECTION: "My Customers Aren't Online"

**Response:**
> "Really? How many people have texted you on WhatsApp this week?"

**Wait for answer.**

> "Those are your customers. They're already on their phones. Already on WhatsApp. They ARE online — just not on YOUR website."

> "When they search 'pizza Asunción' on Google from their phone, what appears? Your competitor's website. They're ready to book. And you're missing them."

---

### OBJECTION: "I Don't Have Time"

**Response:**
> "That's exactly why you need this."

**The time math:**
> "How much time do you spend per day responding to WhatsApp messages about services, prices, and hours?"

**Wait for answer.**

> "Now — how much of that is repetitive? 'What are your hours?' 'Do you have availability Saturday?' 'How much is a color?'"

> "With a website, the answers are RIGHT THERE. Customers see it themselves. Your WhatsApp gets quieter. You get time back."

> "You don't have time NOT to do this."

## TALKING POINTS BY BUSINESS TYPE

---

### For Peluquerías

**The pain:**
> "You know what happens in a busy salon? The WhatsApp gets out of control. 'I'm coming at 3pm' — but they didn't confirm. 'Is 4pm available?' — you respond, but they went to someone else. Sound familiar?"

**The solution:**
> "Online booking puts everything in one place. Customer books, you confirm, everyone knows where they stand. No more confusion, no more double bookings."

**The loyalty angle:**
> "Plus — the loyalty program. Your best clients come back for points. A client who comes 4 times becomes a habit. They stop comparing prices."

---

### For Restaurantes

**The pain:**
> "Phone ringing at dinner time with reservation requests. Trying to remember who's coming, what table they want, if they confirmed. Meanwhile, your real diners are waiting."

**The solution:**
> "Online reservations that show your availability in real-time. Table management built in. No more double bookings, no more 'I didn't get the text.'"

**The menu angle:**
> "And your digital menu — with photos, descriptions, prices. Customers decide before they arrive. They order more, tip better, and come back."

---

### For Gimnasios

**The pain:**
> "Tracking who's paid, who's lapsed, who's coming to what class. Doing it in a notebook or Excel. Getting calls asking 'is the 5pm class full?'"

**The solution:**
> "Members book their own classes. The system tracks who's paid. Automated reminders so people don't forget. Everything organized."

**The retention angle:**
> "The membership tiers give people something to work toward. They move from casual to committed. And loyalty points give them a reason to keep coming."

---

### For Bares/Clubes

**The pain:**
> "Selling tickets at the door. No idea how many people are coming until the night of the event. Last minute chaos."

**The solution:**
> "Online ticket sales with real-time tracking. You know exactly who's coming and what they're buying before the doors open. Preventative for sold-out shows."

**The data angle:**
> "You build a database of everyone who buys tickets. Next event? You know exactly who to reach. Marketing that actually works."

## THE COMPARISON TALKING POINTS

---

### vs. Wix/Squarespace

> "Wix is great for personal projects. But here's the difference:"

**The feature gap:**
> "On Wix, if you want online booking, you pay for a plugin. Loyalty program? Another plugin. Gift cards? Another one. At the end of the month, you've spent G. 100-200k in plugins AND you're still managing them separately."

**The Paraguay gap:**
> "None of those plugins are designed for Paraguay. No Mercado Pago integration. No Guaraní pricing. No WhatsApp integration that actually works."

> "We built this for Paraguay. Everything works together. Everything's in Guaraníes. Your WhatsApp is connected."

---

### vs. Hiring a Developer

> "Hiring a developer is a valid option. But here's what you need to know:"

**The cost:**
> "A custom website in Paraguay starts at G. 3-5 million. Goes up from there. Plus G. 50-100k monthly for maintenance. Plus every time you want a change, you pay again."

**The time:**
> "Three months minimum. Usually six. You're waiting while your competitors move forward."

**The risk:**
> "What happens if your developer disappears? We hear this more than you'd think. You're left with a broken website and no one to fix it."

**Our advantage:**
> "With us, G. 150-400k monthly. No upfront cost. Everything included. Changes when you want. We maintain it."

## THE CLOSING TECHNIQUES

---

### The Two-Choice Close

> "For your business, I recommend Plan Pro at G. 250.000 per month. That gives you everything we talked about. Does that work for your budget, or would you prefer to start with Basic at G. 150.000?"

### The Urgency Close

> "Right now, we're offering free setup for new clients this month. That's G. 100.000 in savings. But only for the next [X] clients."

### The Risk Reversal Close

> "Look — if you sign up today and don't like it after 30 days, I'll refund your first month. No questions. You can keep the website. I just want you to try it."

### The Asymmetry Close

> "Here's the thing: if you do this and it works — which it will — you grow your business. If you don't, you stay exactly where you are. The downside of doing nothing is you keep losing customers to competitors. The downside of trying is... nothing."

### The Social Proof Close

> "50+ businesses in Paraguay are already using this. The average rating is 4.9 stars. 98% satisfaction. At this point, the question isn't whether it works — it's whether you're going to be one of the businesses that has it or one that doesn't."

## PHRASES TO USE

### Build Credibility

- "We're not a startup — we've been doing this for [X years]"
- "50+ businesses in Paraguay"
- "The same system, customized for you"
- "Built specifically for the Paraguayan market"

### Create Urgency

- "Every day without a website, you're losing customers"
- "The opportunity window is open now"
- "Your competitors are making moves"
- "The businesses that started last year have a head start"

### Remove Risk

- "No upfront cost"
- "Month to month, no contracts"
- "30-day money-back guarantee"
- "We maintain everything"

### Show Empathy

- "I know exactly what you're dealing with"
- "We hear this from every [business type] owner"
- "It's the same story we hear all the time"
- "I've seen this pattern in [X businesses]"

---

## PHRASES TO AVOID

- "It's very simple" (sounds condescending)
- "You just need to..." (implies they're not doing enough)
- "No risk at all" (there's always some risk)
- "Everyone is doing this" (not true)
- "This will change your business" (too strong, sounds like a scam)

---

## WHAT TO DO AFTER THE PITCH

1. **Send follow-up message** same day with questionnaire link
2. **Send PDF summary** of pricing and features
3. **Schedule follow-up** call in 3 days
4. **Send testimonial video** if they seem hesitant
5. **Send case study** from similar business type

---

## RED FLAGS — WHEN TO WALK AWAY

These are signs the prospect isn't a good fit:

| Red Flag | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------------|
| Only wants a Facebook page | Not ready for web investment |
| Budget is G. 30k/month | Not sustainable for quality |
| Plans to cancel in 3 months | Won't see results |
| Has unreasonable expectations | Will never be satisfied |
| Comparing to free website builders | Doesn't value professional tools |
| Says "I'll think about it" three times | Not actually interested |
